## Question Bank

> Guidance: conversational order — situation before evaluation before decision. Ask open, then follow the energy; the scripted follow-ups below are for when the first answer stays surface-level. Never defend, never correct, never sell. Every quote captured verbatim with enough context to be quotable later.

### 1. The Situation (their world before the evaluation)

- "Take me back to before you started looking — what was happening that made this a priority?"
- "What were you doing about it at the time? What was that costing you?" *(quantify where they'll go there)*
- "Whose idea was it to look for something? Who else cared?"

### 2. Decision Drivers

> Capture target: the ranked, real criteria — not the RFP's stated ones.

- "When you compared options, what mattered most? What was the real top three?"
- "Was there a moment where the decision tipped? What happened?"
- "What did your [CFO / director / board] need to see before they'd sign off?"
- "If [chosen option] hadn't existed, what would you have done?"

### 3. Alternatives Considered

> Capture target: the real consideration set, including build-it-ourselves and do-nothing, and how we were positioned against each — in their words, not ours.

- "Who else did you look at seriously? Who made the final two?"
- "What did [competitor] do well in the process? Where did they fall short?"
- "How would you describe the difference between us and [competitor] to a peer?" *(this exact phrasing produces battlecard-grade language)*
- "Was staying with your current approach ever the leading option? What kept it alive / killed it?"

### 4. Buyer Language Capture

> Capture target: raw vocabulary for `GTM-War-Room/BRAND-DNA/our-customer.md` — "what they say vs. what they feel." Do not paraphrase into our messaging; the divergence between their words and our copy is the finding.

- "In your own words — what problem were you actually solving?"
- "How did you describe this project internally when you asked for budget?"
- "What almost made you walk away from us?" *(objection language, verbatim)*
- "What would you tell someone in your role at another [agency/company] evaluating this?"

### 5. Trust Signals

> Capture target: what made belief happen (or fail) — references, proof, compliance, people. Trust signals feed the foundational doc §8 and the persona files.

- "At what point did you believe we could actually do this? What did you see?"
- "Did you talk to any of our customers? What did that change?"
- "Was there anything in our materials or process that gave you pause?"
- "[If lost] What did [winner] show you that we didn't?"

### 6. Close

- "What should we have asked that we didn't?"
- "If we made one change based on this conversation, what should it be?"

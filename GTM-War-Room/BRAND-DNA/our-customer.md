# Our Customer — how capital program owners actually talk

Raw customer language, not summaries. Source basis: composite personas built from Aurigo customer discovery (n=94 interviews, 2024–2026, per the engineering playbook) plus the approved messaging doc. **No individually attributed customer quotes are cleared for external use yet** — treat every line here as internal raw material until the Customer Evidence agent clears named quotes.

## What they say (composite, internal-only)

- "We can't afford to break the work order system." — stated selection reason, metro transit authority (Hybrid-mode deal; internal record).
- "Why this bridge? Why now? Why $8 million? What happens if we wait five years?" — the questions a county engineer faces from elected officials.
- "If the council asked next Tuesday for a condition report on the city's infrastructure, what would we show them?" — city public works framing; the honest answer today is "we're not sure," and the political cost of that answer is severe.
- "When the senior bridge inspector retires, everything he knows walks out the door." — institutional-knowledge fear, counties and DOTs alike.
- "The TAMP takes us six to twelve months of manual data aggregation, and it's outdated the day we publish it." — state DOT asset managers.
- "Finance cut our replacement list by 30% because we couldn't back it up. Sometimes they cut the wrong items." — manufacturing operations.
- "AI just makes things up — I can't trust it for funding calls." / "We can't put our data into an AI model." / "This is just a chatbot." — live AI objections (counters in personas.md and the messaging doc D4).

## What they feel underneath

- **Fear of being unable to answer.** The legislator, the board, the council, the auditor will ask, and the data is in six systems or one retiring head.
- **Risk aversion beats feature appetite (public owners).** A procurement officer choosing between IBM and Aurigo faces personal risk if the smaller vendor stumbles. Perceived vendor risk, not functional evaluation, drives roughly a third of top-10 DOT losses.
- **Capacity squeeze.** Programs are growing faster than headcount; expertise is retiring. They do not want fewer people; they want more program per person ("do more with the same people").

## Decision drivers

- **Public owners:** federal funding eligibility and compliance (TAMP/23 CFR 515, NBIS, NHPP, FTA TAM); audit and transparency pressure; peer-agency references; auditability of every decision; low implementation risk (integrate, don't rip-and-replace).
- **Facility owners:** P&L accountability — downtime cost ($50K–$500K/hour manufacturing; $9K/minute per data center SLA breach), OEE, uptime SLA, regulatory documentation (FDA 21 CFR Part 11, PHMSA, NERC CIP). ROI arguments work here; never use "ROI" with public owners — say program outcomes or capital program performance.

## Urgency and buying triggers

New bond or capital funding program; audit/compliance pressure (18 states carry active FHWA corrective action findings); leadership turnover and knowledge loss; rising backlog, delays, or overruns; IIJA reporting requirements on a fixed authorization window.

## Approval process

- **Public owners:** formal, rule-bound, risk-averse. Needs identification → market research (AASHTO, APWA, TRB conferences) → 50–100-page RFP → evaluation and references → board/legislative approval → implementation. Total cycle 18–36 months. Peer references are the accelerant: one successful DOT deployment presented at AASHTO seeds the next RFP.
- **Facility owners:** P&L buyer (VP Operations, Director of Critical Infrastructure), 6–18 month cycles, technically rigorous procurement, faster where downtime pain is quantified.

## Trust signals

Named peer outcomes (Utah DOT $19.5M/year; Las Vegas 3+ months staff time/year; Wisconsin DOT 15,000 projects); FedRAMP-Class C Certified platform, ISO 42001 in progress; cited answers ("no source, no answer") and full audit trails; human-in-the-loop on every agent action; 90-day time-to-value with a real report produced from their own data; nothing leaves the tenant, nothing trains public models.

## Buyer vocabulary (use their words)

- **Public owners:** capital program, CIP, STIP, TAMP, NBIS/NBI, PCI, IRI, federal-aid, obligation deadlines, right of way, RFI, change orders, structurally deficient, state of good repair, end users (never "clients"), program (never "portfolio").
- **Facility owners:** capital portfolio, OEE, unplanned downtime, uptime SLA / Tier III, PUE, IQ/OQ/PQ, requalification, fitness-for-service, asset integrity, ROI.
- **Caution:** buyers say "defensible" and "on time, on budget" constantly; Aurigo copy should not (per voice standards — use "transparent and accountable program delivery," "keep projects on track"). Record their words; translate in ours.

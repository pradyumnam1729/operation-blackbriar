# Brand Voice — Aurigo (operational condensate)

Full reference: `Voice of Aurigo - Standards Reference.md` (repo root; condensed from second-brain/voice/voice-of-aurigo-profile.md). This file is the working summary injected into every session. When in doubt, the full reference wins.

## The cardinal rule

Every piece of Aurigo content opens **from the reader's world** — their decision point, their constraint, their risk, their situation right now. Not from Aurigo. Not from the product. Not from industry context-setting. Everything else follows from this.

Companion rule from the Masterworks AI messaging doc: **the customer is the hero, Aurigo is the guide.** Write "Agencies use Masterworks AI to... (outcome)," never "Masterworks AI does..."

## The six constants (never change, any surface)

| Be this | Not this |
|---------|----------|
| Conversational — a trusted advisor who knows the room | Corporate — a brochure |
| Knowledgeable — domain command: capital programs, CIP, right of way, RFI, change orders | Jargon-heavy — terms dropped to sound expert |
| Confident — direct claims about what Aurigo does | Hedged — "may," "could," "might," "potentially" |
| Direct — short sentences, active voice, clear subject | Passive — buried leads, roundabout constructions |
| Reader-first — open from their decision, their world | Product-first — open from the company or feature |
| Precise — name the outcome: on-time delivery, audit readiness, budget adherence | Vague — "better outcomes," "greater efficiency" |

## Tone by surface (voice constant, tone shifts)

| Surface | Tone | Sentences |
|---------|------|-----------|
| Hero / above the fold | Bold, declarative, outcome-focused | Very short |
| Product pages | Authoritative, specific, benefit-first | Short–medium |
| Feature descriptions | Clear, practical, no flourish | Short |
| FAQ | Direct, natural language, complete sentences | Medium |
| Social proof / stats | Confident, grounded, no filler | Very short |
| Thought leadership (bylined) | SME's voice + Aurigo standard as floor; confirm the named author | Medium–long |
| Blog (SEO) | Advisory, practical; question-format H1, direct answer in first 50 words | Short–medium |
| Sales collateral | Persuasive, outcome-specific, persona-matched | Mixed |
| Email (external) | Advisory; one thread, one action; reader's situation in the opening | Short–medium |
| Press releases | Third person, factual, AP style | Mixed |

## Per-channel voice profiles

- **Website (Enterprise voice):** buyer pain visible, outcome leads, product named correctly for the vertical, specific trust anchor. AI-visible structure (clean standalone facts an LLM can cite).
- **Sales one-pager (Enterprise):** audience-specific outcomes, correct product name, one specific proof point.
- **Nurture email (Advisory):** one CTA, insight before product mention.
- **In-product / careers (Human voice):** specific over generic, no cheerful corporate filler; tooltips start with a verb, errors state problem and fix without blame.
- **LinkedIn / social:** outcome or insight first; no launch-day blitz without validated messaging.

## Hard rules (see gtm-rules.md for the enforced list)

- Banned buzzwords are machine-enforced by hook. Also: no filler closers, no "circle back," American English only ("learn more," "toward," American spellings).
- "AI-native" is the only approved AI modifier. Name agents specifically (Risk Prediction Agent, Funding Agent). "Masterworks AI, powered by Lumina" is the only approved "powered by" construction.
- Binary contrast framing ("This is not X. It is Y.") maximum once per piece. Em dashes: one to two per page in external content.
- Lead with What and Why, not How. Answer "what does this do for me?" before technology.

## Six tests before anything ships

1. **Reader test** — first line opens from the reader's world, not Aurigo.
2. **Specificity test** — every claim backed by a number, named product, named audience, or concrete outcome.
3. **Banned phrase test** — nothing from the forbidden list.
4. **Active voice test** — every sentence has a clear subject doing the action.
5. **AI visibility test** — an LLM could pull a clean, accurate, standalone summary.
6. **Swap test** — replace "Aurigo" with Oracle, Microsoft, or Kahua; if the sentence still works, rewrite until it is irreplaceably Aurigo.

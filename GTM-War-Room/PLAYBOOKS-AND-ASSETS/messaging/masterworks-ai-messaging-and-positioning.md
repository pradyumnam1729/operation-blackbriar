# Masterworks AI — Positioning & Messaging

## A1 · The Why (Golden Circle)

**Why**
The product exists to complete the capital project life cycle, which Aurigo does not currently cover end-to-end. Because in DOTs projects never truly die — remedy decisions come from asset condition — it lets owners automate and digitize that part of the process into a single platform (Masterworks), closing the loop from condition back to capital planning and delivery.

**How**
Masterworks AI is built on Lumina AI, Aurigo's AI platform, grounded in more than 20 years of capital-program data (it understands fund sources, apportionments, and change orders). Because the AI runs inside the system of action — the customer's own Masterworks tenant — no data leaves the workflow, and every recommendation is explainable, permissioned, and auditable. It cites every source ("if there's no source, there's no answer"), logs every agent action in a full audit trail, and routes actions to a person for approval ("agents prepare work, your people approve it"). Built on a FedRAMP-Class C Certified platform, so nothing trains public models.

**What**
Masterworks AI brings Copilot and ready-to-use agents directly into the workflows teams use to plan, deliver, and maintain capital programs — so they can find answers, predict risk, automate routine work, and take the next best action without leaving Masterworks. It runs inside the customer's tenant on the Lumina platform, gives teams cited answers from their own program data, and ships with seven agents available out of the box.

## A2 · Market & Category

**Category**
AI for capital program management — purpose-built for public infrastructure and facility owners. It is deliberately distinct from generic AI assistants and bolt-on copilots. As buyers put it: "Bolt-ons read generic data," while Lumina is grounded in 20+ years of capital-program data and acts inside the system of action; and "pilots fail when the AI sits beside the system of record. This runs inside the system of action."

**Why now**
Three shifts make this urgent: complexity has outrun the tools (bigger portfolios, more funding rules, more stakeholders); expertise is walking out the door as veteran planners and engineers retire; and data is siloed across project, funding, contract, and asset systems, so critical signals surface too late to act on. In the field this shows up as a newly approved bond program growing the portfolio "roughly forty percent over the next two bienniums" while headcount stays flat, three senior planners retiring within eighteen months, audit findings citing scattered documentation, and staff already "pasting contract data into free chatbots because we gave them nothing sanctioned."

**Market context**
Two unverified sets of figures exist. Product document: the construction AI market is projected at $4.5B by 2026, with AI deployments reporting 15–20% forecast-accuracy gains and 10–15% reduction in planning effort. Customer transcript (internal research, no third-party source or date): every four years DOTs pay approximately $500K to $3 million just to get the TAMP released.

## A3 · Best-Fit Customer & ICP

**Best fit**
Public infrastructure and facility owners running active, funded capital programs who need to do more with lean teams while staying auditable and in control. Concretely, a state DOT or city public works capital program office with a newly funded or growing program, flat headcount, retiring senior staff, a need for every decision to be auditable, and a strict IT policy against data entering public models.

**Segments**
Federal agencies; state and large local government agencies. Transcripts show this in practice — a state DOT capital program office and a city public works finance and delivery organization are the engaged buyers, with Federal Highways / Federal Lands named as a near-term buyer (in the asset/Maintain discussion rather than a Masterworks AI call).

**Who's in the room**
Capital Program / PMO Director (economic buyer); Finance & Budget Officer; Project / Portfolio Manager (user); and IT & Security. In the calls these appear as Capital Program Director (Dana R., Meridian DOT) and PMO Lead (Chris T.), Finance & Budget Officer (Maria L.), IT Security Manager (Sam K.), and Deputy Director of Capital Delivery (Priya N.). The Capital Program Director and Finance & Budget Officer act as economic buyers — Dana controls new money, Maria must approve the finance office — while IT Security is the gatekeeper who can block on data residency.

**Buying triggers**
A new bond or capital funding program; audit, transparency, or compliance pressure; leadership turnover and knowledge loss; and rising backlog, delays, or overruns. In the calls this shows up as "the legislature just approved a new bond program... three of my most senior planners retiring inside eighteen months," an audit finding that "decisions were sound, documentation was scattered," and recurring board-season demands for data pulls that consume staff time.

**Not a fit**
No active capital program or funding source; content with spreadsheets and no executive pressure; cloud or AI blocked by agency policy; and no budget cycle within the next 12 months. Small agencies are also explicitly out of ICP — "there's a low appetite for cost. These agencies do not, would not want to pay any higher price" — making the bottom-end market cheap, slow, and dispersed, and very difficult to sell to.

## A4 · Competitive Alternatives & Right to Win

**Alternatives**
Buyers rely on systems of record that capture work but cannot help teams anticipate or act, with project, funding, contract, and asset data living in disconnected systems. In practice that means spreadsheets, per-PM status kept separately, and manual multi-system data hunts — "someone spends two days pulling from the project system, the funding spreadsheet, and whatever's in the contract files. Half the answer is in scanned PDFs." Other alternatives: a prior generic AI assistant pilot that sat outside systems and "made things up," waiting for the incumbent to bolt AI onto existing tools, and shadow AI — "staff pasting contract data into free chatbots." The product is positioned against generic AI assistants and bolt-on copilots.

**What they can't copy**
Domain-grounded in 20+ years of capital-program data (it understands fund sources, apportionments, and change orders); runs inside the system of action within existing workflows with no data export; governed, explainable, and human-in-the-loop with full audit trails; and 7 agents live plus Agent Builder. Buyers hear it as cited answers with "no source, no answer," and a FedRAMP-Class C Certified platform where "data does not leave the workflow. Nothing — your data never trains public models."

**Proof it's real**
300+ owner organizations and $300B+ in capital programs managed on Aurigo. Utah DOT saves $19.5M/year (and projects $100M in five-year savings); Las Vegas Public Works freed up 3+ months of staff time per year; Wisconsin DOT migrated 15,000 projects; Tampa Bay Water District serves 2.3M citizens. Seven agents available out of the box.

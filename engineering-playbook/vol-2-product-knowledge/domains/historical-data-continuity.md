# Historical Data Continuity & Cycle Architecture

> Volume 2 · Product Knowledge · Domain  
> Status: **COMMITTEE REVIEW REQUIRED — Critical gap raised by VP Products, July 2026**  
> Applies to: Masterworks Maintain (TAMP cycles) and Primus Maintain (fiscal / ISO cycles)

---

## The VP Products Question — What It Really Means

The question was: *"How can the product deal in a cyclic way? What about the data historically generated by the customers — historical TAMP reports, etc.?"*

This is not a feature request. It is a fundamental architectural question about whether the product has a mental model of time. The current architecture treats the world as a snapshot: assets have a current condition, a current RUL, a current capital plan. There is no concept of cycles, no concept of prior commitments, no model of how data accumulates and carries forward across planning periods.

This is fine for a demo. It is fatal for a production product used by a State DOT on a 4-year TAMP cycle.

Here is the failure mode: A State DOT buys Aurigo in Year 1 of their TAMP cycle. They use it to produce their TAMP. Four years later, they start the next TAMP cycle. FHWA asks: "Show us your performance against the targets you committed to in the prior cycle." Aurigo has no answer. The prior cycle's targets, the prior cycle's capital plan, the prior cycle's condition baseline — all of it is either overwritten or unreachable. The DOT has to go back to their old Excel files. They do not renew.

The cycle problem is the retention problem. Solving it is what turns Aurigo from a one-cycle tool into a 20-year platform relationship.

## Two Dimensions of the Cycle Problem

### Dimension 1: Intra-Cycle Data Accumulation (within a single planning period)

Within a 4-year TAMP cycle, data does not sit still:
- Inspection rounds happen annually (pavement) and biennially (NBIS bridges)
- Treatment events change the condition trajectory mid-cycle
- Capital investments funded in Year 1 should update the model's projection for Years 2–4
- Budget appropriations change — the financial plan needs to reflect actual appropriations vs. the plan
- FHWA issues revised guidance mid-cycle (this happened with BIL/extreme weather in 2023)

The product must maintain a time-series of condition observations within the cycle — not just the latest condition value. Every update must be timestamped, author-attributed, and linked to the source inspection or event. The condition trend within a cycle is a leading indicator: a DOT whose pavement condition is declining faster than the model predicted in Year 1 needs to know this in Year 2, not at the end of the cycle.

### Dimension 2: Cycle-to-Cycle Continuity (what carries forward)

When a TAMP cycle closes and a new one opens, six things must carry forward — and only one of them (the asset inventory) is handled by the current architecture:

| What Carries Forward | Current Behavior | Required Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Asset inventory (with updates) | ✅ Exists (assets persist) | ✅ OK |
| Performance targets committed to FHWA in prior cycle | ❌ Not stored | Store as `cycle_target` records; immutable after submission |
| Actual investment vs. plan from prior cycle | ❌ Not tracked | Track funded vs. planned by year; produce performance report |
| Deterioration model parameters calibrated from prior cycle data | ❌ Always defaults | Weibull β/λ parameters update after each cycle with actual inspection history |
| Treatment cost actuals from prior cycle | ❌ Not tracked | Import from Build/EAM; calibrate unit cost library |
| Unfunded capital needs backlog from prior cycle | ❌ Overwritten | Carry forward as backlog; show age and growth of deferred need |

Missing any one of these breaks the FHWA consistency determination. Missing all five means the product has no memory.

## The Historical Data Problem at Customer Onboarding

Every new customer arrives with a history Aurigo knows nothing about.

A typical State DOT onboarding in 2026:
- Their oldest interstate bridge was constructed in 1968 (58-year asset with 40 years of NBI inspection records in BrM)
- Their pavement network has 15 years of IRI measurements in HPMS annual submissions
- They have 3 prior TAMP documents (2014, 2018, 2022) with committed targets, investment strategies, and condition baselines
- They have 10 years of treatment records: which lane-miles were mill-and-overlayed when, at what unit cost, by which contractor
- Their current TAMP cycle started in 2022; the next cycle update is due in 2026

Without this historical data:
1. **Weibull models run on national defaults** — a DOT in Louisiana (extreme heat, freeze-thaw, hurricane-driven emergency repairs) gets the same deterioration curve as a DOT in Arizona. Capital needs projections are wrong by years, not months.
2. **Year-of-need projections are meaningless** — if we don't know an asset's 2018 condition + 2021 condition + 2024 condition, we cannot fit a deterioration curve. We can only extrapolate forward from today, which gives a straight line, not the actual curve.
3. **TAMP cannot show prior-cycle performance** — FHWA asks: "You committed to ≤5% Poor in 2022. What did you actually achieve?" Aurigo cannot answer.
4. **Capital needs are not calibrated to actual costs** — the unit cost library uses national RS Means data. Actual costs in the state may be 30–60% higher (Hawaii, Alaska) or 15–20% lower (rural midwest). Without treatment cost history, estimates are systematically wrong.
5. **The first TAMP produced by Aurigo will be rejected** — an investment strategy narrative that cannot cite longitudinal condition trends and cannot compare to prior targets will fail FHWA review.

### Minimum Viable Historical Data (what we must accept at onboarding)

| Data Type | Minimum | Ideal | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asset installation/construction year | Required | — | Asset record or legacy system |
| Last major treatment date + type | Required | — | Treatment record or agency memory |
| Current condition score | Required | — | Latest inspection |
| 3+ historical condition scores with dates | High value | All inspection history | BrM export, HPMS flat file, PMS export |
| Treatment history (type, date, cost) | High value | Full history | EAM (Maximo), Cityworks, Build |
| Prior TAMP documents (PDF or Word) | High value | — | Agency document archive |
| Prior TAMP performance targets (structured) | High value | — | Extracted from TAMP PDF or entered manually |
| Historical STIP/TIP project lists | Medium | — | State DOT STIP database or FHWA STIP |
| Unit cost actuals from completed projects | Medium | — | Build project records |

The minimum viable historical data (installation year + last treatment + current condition) is available for virtually every asset from agency records or engineering judgment. Getting this minimum enables Weibull calibration with 3 data points: initial condition (assumed good at installation), post-treatment condition (assumed good at last treatment), and current condition. This is enough to fit the curve and produce defensible RUL projections.

## The Cyclic Product Design: What Must Be Built

### 1. Planning Cycle as a First-Class Entity

Every asset, inspection, capital need, and performance target must belong to a **planning cycle**. A planning cycle is a named, bounded time period with start date, end date, committed performance targets, and a status (active, archived, superseded).

```
PlanningCycle
  id, tenant_id
  name              -- "TAMP 2022–2026", "FY2026 Capital Plan", "ISO 55001 Cycle 3"
  cycle_type        -- TampFederal | FiscalYear | IsoCycle | Custom
  start_date
  end_date
  status            -- Draft | Active | ConsistencyDetermination | Archived
  submitted_date    -- date submitted to FHWA (Masterworks) or board (Primus)
  fhwa_accepted_date -- date of FHWA consistency determination letter

CycleTarget
  id, tenant_id, cycle_id
  asset_class       -- Pavement | Bridge | Culvert | etc.
  metric            -- IRI_GoodPercent | StructurallyDeficientDeckPercent | etc.
  target_value
  fhwa_minimum      -- the federal floor (non-negotiable)
  committed_value   -- what the DOT committed to above the minimum
  target_year       -- the year by which this target must be achieved
  is_locked         -- true after FHWA submission; immutable

CycleActual
  id, tenant_id, cycle_id
  asset_class, metric
  measurement_date
  actual_value
  source            -- Inspection | HpmsSubmission | NbiSubmission | Manual
  variance_from_target -- calculated: actual_value - committed_value
```

When a cycle is archived, its targets become immutable baselines for the next cycle. The new cycle's narrative engine automatically cites: "In the 2022–2026 cycle, we committed to ≤5% Interstate pavement Poor. The 2026 HPMS submission shows 3.8% Poor — target exceeded."

### 2. Historical Data Ingestion Pipeline

A structured ETL pipeline for importing historical data into Aurigo at onboarding and on an ongoing basis:

**BrM / AASHTOWare Export Ingestion**
- Accept the standard BrM export format (XML or CSV) with NBI fields
- Map NBI items 58/59/60 to Aurigo condition score
- Import bridge inspection history as time-series condition records, each linked to a historical planning cycle (or a `pre_aurigo_history` bucket for data that predates Aurigo use)
- Preserve inspector name, inspection date, NBI rating — these fields are auditable by FHWA

**HPMS Flat File Ingestion**
- Accept the HPMS standard format (the same format the state DOT sends to FHWA annually)
- Map HPMS data elements to pavement asset records and condition records
- Import multiple years of HPMS submissions as separate condition snapshots
- This gives immediate multi-year condition history for pavement assets

**Prior TAMP Document Ingestion (AI-Assisted)**
- Accept TAMP PDF or Word document upload
- AI extraction (Claude Sonnet): pull performance targets, investment strategy decisions, condition baselines, and financial plan assumptions into structured form
- Human review step: user confirms or corrects extracted values before they become CycleTarget records
- The prior TAMP becomes the baseline for the current cycle — FHWA can see continuity

**Treatment History Import**
- CSV import format for treatment records: asset ID, treatment type, treatment date, unit cost, quantity, total cost, funding source
- Or: pull from connected EAM (Maximo work order records with completion date + cost roll-up)
- Treatment events populate the asset's lifecycle history and calibrate the unit cost library
- A mill-and-overlay applied in 2019 at $48.50/lane-mile tells the model more about this state's costs than any national RS Means table

**Condition History Bulk Import**
- CSV format: asset ID, condition score, inspection date, inspector name, scale (IRI/NBI/PASER/Custom)
- Validation: flag impossible sequences (condition improved from 2.0 to 4.5 without a recorded treatment)
- Import creates `historical_condition_record` entries linked to assets, with `data_origin = Import`
- These records feed the Weibull calibration engine immediately

### 3. Deterioration Model Calibration from Historical Data

The current Weibull engine uses national defaults when fewer than 3 inspection points exist in Aurigo. With the historical import pipeline, this changes:

**Calibration algorithm (updated):**
1. Collect all condition records for an asset (historical imports + Aurigo-recorded)
2. If ≥ 3 data points exist: fit Weibull β/λ parameters using least-squares minimization
3. If < 3 data points but installation year + last treatment + current condition exist: use 3-point fit (initial condition = 4.8 assumed at construction/treatment; current condition = actual; time elapsed = known)
4. If none of the above: use asset class defaults from the national library (same as today)

**Calibration is cycle-aware:**
After each TAMP cycle, the calibration reruns on the accumulated data. Each successive cycle produces better-calibrated models for that tenant. After 3 cycles (12 years), a state DOT's deterioration models are calibrated entirely from their own historical experience — national defaults become irrelevant.

**Calibration confidence score:**
Display a model confidence indicator on the RUL projection: "Model calibrated from 14 historical data points (state-specific)" vs. "Model uses national defaults (insufficient history — import historical inspection data to improve)". This prompts customers to provide historical data rather than accepting defaults.

### 4. Cycle-to-Cycle Comparison Engine

When a new TAMP cycle begins, the product produces a **Cycle Performance Report** — the document that FHWA reviewers use for the consistency determination:

```
Cycle Performance Report (2022–2026)
─────────────────────────────────────
Asset Class: NHS Pavement
  2022 Commitment: ≤5% Poor (Interstate), ≤10% Poor (Non-Interstate NHS)
  2026 Actual:      3.8% Poor (Interstate), 8.1% Poor (Non-Interstate NHS)
  Status: ✅ Both targets met

Asset Class: NHS Bridges
  2022 Commitment: ≤8% Structurally Deficient deck area
  2026 Actual:      9.2% Structurally Deficient
  Status: ⚠️ Target missed by 1.2 percentage points
  Required Action: Investment strategy narrative must explain variance
                   and document corrective action in 2026–2030 cycle

Capital Investment (2022–2026)
  Planned:   $1.84B (from 2022 TAMP financial plan)
  Actual:    $1.61B (from STIP project records)
  Variance:  -$230M (12.5% shortfall)
  Deferred:  23 bridge deck replacements + 187 pavement lane-miles
  Carryforward backlog: $312M (deferred need + deterioration during deferral)
```

This report is generated automatically from cycle records + STIP project data + condition measurements. It eliminates 3–6 weeks of consultant work from every TAMP cycle.

### 5. Longitudinal Asset Health Timeline

Each asset gets a **lifetime view** — a horizontal timeline showing:
- Construction / installation date
- All recorded condition measurements (imported + Aurigo)
- All treatment events (type, cost, result)
- All planning cycle assignments
- Projected condition trajectory (with confidence interval)
- Prior TAMP cycle target lines overlaid

This visualization is the most important thing FHWA reviewers need to see for individual bridge and pavement segment audits. It makes the asset's entire history legible at a glance.

For Primus assets (manufacturing equipment, UPS units, etc.): the same timeline shows OEE trends, MTBF history, maintenance events, and capital interventions — giving the VP of Operations the asset's full operational biography before making a replacement decision.

## Applicability to Primus (Private Sector)

The cyclic architecture is not Masterworks-only. Every Primus vertical has its own cycle structure:

| Vertical | Cycle Type | Cycle Length | Governing Framework |
|---|---|---|---|
| Manufacturing | Annual capital budget + 3-5 year strategic plan | 1 / 3-5 years | Board approval cycle |
| Data Centers | Equipment lifecycle phases (battery replacement, major refresh) | 5–7 / 15 years | Uptime Institute Tier standards |
| Life Sciences | Equipment qualification cycles (initial → requalification) | 3–5 years | FDA 21 CFR Part 11, EU Annex 11 |
| Utilities | Reliability reporting cycle + asset replacement program | 1 / 5 years | NERC CIP, FERC |

For Life Sciences specifically: the requalification cycle is a regulatory compliance forcing function analogous to the FHWA consistency determination. If a piece of validated equipment exceeds its requalification interval without documented revalidation, it is non-compliant. Aurigo must track this cycle the same way it tracks the TAMP consistency determination.

For Manufacturing: the cycle problem manifests as "economic replacement age" — the model predicts Year X as replacement-optimal, but the actual capital investment happened in Year X+3 due to budget deferral. The product needs to track this deferral, update the deterioration model for the extra 3 years of operation, and recalculate the total cost of the deferral (capital inflation + increased maintenance cost + production loss).

## Impact on Current Roadmap

### What the VP Products Feedback Means for MVP Scope

The MVP as currently scoped produces a TAMP from current data with no historical context. This is a demo-quality TAMP. A TAMP submitted to FHWA without historical context and without prior-cycle target comparison will fail review.

**MVP must be updated to include (minimum):**
1. Asset installation year + last treatment date as required fields (not optional)
2. Historical condition import (CSV bulk upload with date-stamped condition records)
3. The `PlanningCycle` entity (even if only one cycle exists at first use)
4. Prior TAMP target entry (manual input of prior cycle committed targets)

**Without these four items, the MVP cannot produce a FHWA-compliant TAMP.** A non-compliant TAMP is not an MVP for a State DOT — it is a prototype that fails at its primary success criterion.

### What Must Move to Beta from the Current GA Queue

The following items are currently in GA scope (or not scoped at all) but must move to **Beta** given the cycle architecture requirement:

1. **BrM/AASHTOWare import** — currently scoped as a medium-term integration; must be Beta because every state DOT needs it before they can produce a calibrated TAMP
2. **HPMS flat file ingestion** — not currently scoped; must be Beta
3. **Weibull calibration from historical data** — currently defaults to national averages indefinitely; must be Beta (the current behavior makes capital needs projections wrong for the first 2–3 years of every deployment)
4. **Cycle Performance Report** — not currently scoped; must be Beta (this is the FHWA consistency determination artifact)
5. **Prior TAMP AI extractor** — not currently scoped; high-value Beta feature (eliminates 2–3 weeks of manual target entry)

## Competitive Implication

No competitor in the market has solved the cycle architecture problem for TAMP:
- Trimble Unity Maintain (AgileAssets): produces lifecycle plans but does not manage cycle-to-cycle continuity or historical import
- IBM Maximo AIP: has a good health-score time series for equipment, but no TAMP cycle concept and no federal compliance framing
- Consulting firms (HNTB, WSP): solve cycle continuity manually — they carry the institutional memory in consultants' files and heads

**The first vendor to ship cycle architecture wins the renewal.** Every current TAMP tool is a one-cycle engagement. Aurigo can be a 20-year platform relationship if the cycle architecture is right.

## Open Questions for the Committee

The committee needs to resolve four design decisions before sprint planning:

**Q1: Where does historical data live — same table or separate?**
Option A: Historical imports go into the same `condition_records` table with a `data_origin = Import` flag. Simple, but risks polluting current operational data with historical imports of uncertain quality.  
Option B: Separate `historical_condition_records` table. Historical data feeds the calibration engine and timeline view but does not appear in operational dashboards unless the user explicitly includes it.  
**Recommendation: Option B.** The quality of historical imports is lower than Aurigo-recorded data. Keep them separate, use them for calibration, expose them in timeline views. A failed import batch cannot corrupt the current operational view.

**Q2: How do we handle pre-Aurigo cycles?**
A DOT onboarding in 2026 has 2 prior TAMP cycles (2014–2018, 2018–2022). Do we create cycle records for those periods?  
**Recommendation: Yes, create `pre_aurigo_cycle` records.** Users enter the prior cycle targets manually (or AI-extracts them from uploaded TAMP PDFs). These records populate the cycle comparison view and give FHWA reviewers the longitudinal picture. They are read-only; no capital needs are generated from them.

**Q3: What is the handoff trigger from one cycle to the next?**
Option A: Time-based — automatically opens new cycle when the old one's end date passes.  
Option B: User-triggered — Agency Admin explicitly closes the current cycle and opens the next.  
**Recommendation: Option B, with a time-based reminder.** TAMP cycles can extend, FHWA can grant extensions, and some agencies may want to keep a cycle open while under FHWA review. The close/open actions must be human decisions with explicit confirmation. The system prompts with a "Cycle Closing" checklist (targets locked, consistency determination uploaded, prior cycle archived).

**Q4: Do Primus cycles use the same entity as Masterworks TAMP cycles?**
**Recommendation: Yes — same `PlanningCycle` entity with a `cycle_type` discriminator.** The cycle mechanics (start/end, targets, actuals, archive, carryforward) are identical. Only the target metric definitions and compliance templates differ. Building separate cycle systems for Masterworks and Primus would create two maintenance burdens for one concept.

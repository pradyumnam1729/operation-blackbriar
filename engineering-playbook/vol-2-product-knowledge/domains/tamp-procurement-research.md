## Section 1: TAMP Renewal Calendar

### The Cycle Structure

FHWA certifies TAMP development processes at least every 4 years (23 CFR Part 515). The first generation of state TAMPs was certified in 2018–2019 under MAP-21. The Bipartisan Infrastructure Law (BIL) required all states to update their TAMPs to incorporate extreme weather/resilience considerations; FHWA gave states until December 31, 2022 to submit BIL-compliant updates. The bulk of state TAMPs on the FHWA plans page are therefore 2022 vintage, making 2026 the next major renewal cycle.

### Market Structure Observation

Two firms dominate the confirmed assignments: **Applied Pavement Technology (APTech)** (NY, KY, AK, NV, ND, OH) and **Gannett Fleming (GFT)** (NJ, RI). Both are pavement-specialist firms, not general transportation engineering consultants. This is the opposite of the conventional assumption that HNTB, WSP, AECOM, or Jacobs hold TAMP contracts.

The large general engineering firms (HNTB, WSP, AECOM, Jacobs) may hold TAMP work embedded within broader on-call contracts (see Stantec's $1.7M NYSDOT on-call), but the dedicated TAMP specialists are smaller pavement engineering firms. This has significant implications for Aurigo's sales strategy: the software displaces specialist boutique firms more than large ENR Top 500 firms.

**Source note**: No press releases from HNTB, WSP, AECOM, or Jacobs specifically advertising state DOT TAMP contracts were found via web search. This absence is itself evidence. These firms do broader transportation planning work but are not visibly the TAMP specialists.

## Section 3: Cost Breakdown — What the $500K–$3M Buys

### Evidence Base for the Cost Range

The playbook's $500K–$3M range is industry lore. The following is the closest evidence found via web research:

**Tennessee DOT Gap Analysis (FHWA)**: Referenced a cost-tier framework: low = under $100K; medium = $100K–$500K; high = over $500K for engaging a consultant on a specific TAMP improvement initiative. This implies a single-task engagement at $100K–$500K and a full TAMP at well over $500K.  
**Source**: https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/asset/gap/tngap.pdf (document binary, text not extractable by web tool, but referenced in search result excerpt)

**NJDOT TAMP (Gannett Fleming)**: Multiple TAMP cycles (2018, 2019, 2022, 2024 annual review) as prime consultant. No contract value disclosed publicly.

**NYSDOT TAMP (APTech, 2021–2022)**: 20-month engagement, no cost disclosed.

**Stantec / NYSDOT on-call**: $1,746,023 for a 4-year on-call asset management contract (Nov 2024–Dec 2028). This is not exclusively TAMP production — it is on-call support — but it is the only confirmed dollar figure in the public record for a TAMP-adjacent consulting engagement at a 9-state DOT.  
**Source**: NY OSC contract D041155, referenced in search results.

**Seattle Public Utilities (utility, not DOT)**: Phased asset management consulting: Phase 1 estimated $100K–$250K; all phases estimated $1M–$2M.  
**Source**: https://consultants.seattle.gov/2020/02/05/asset-management-support-services-spu-rfp-contract-19-182-s/ — comparable in structure but not a DOT TAMP.

**Total scale**: For a large-state DOT (NY, PA, TX, CA), the playbook's $1M–$3M figure is consistent with 16–24 months of a 2–4 person consulting team. For smaller states, a 12–16 month engagement at 1–2 FTE produces the $500K–$1M range. No public audited cost data was found; these figures are inferred from scope evidence and contract durations.

### DOT Internal Staff Cost vs. Consultant Cost

No published ratio was found in web-accessible documents. Based on the evidence:

- Caltrans 2026 TAMP appears to be **primarily internal staff** with supporting workshops — no external prime consultant was identified. Caltrans has a dedicated Asset Management office with internal staff capacity.
- NYSDOT engaged APTech as **external prime** for the 2022 TAMP, with internal Kevin Zynda (Supervisor of Asset Management) as the client-side coordinator.
- MnDOT's 2014 TAMP used "outside contractors" (APTech) for specific analytical tasks while internal work groups handled data collection and financial planning — a **hybrid model**.
- GFT's NJDOT engagement describes GFT as "Prime Consultant," implying NJDOT internal staff in a client-review role only.

**Discovery question for Sales**: When approaching a state DOT, ask whether they intend to produce the 2026 TAMP internally (Caltrans model) or through an external prime (NYSDOT model). The answer determines whether Aurigo competes with the consulting firm or alongside it.

### AASHTO TAM Guide Definition of Roles

Per the AASHTO TAM Guide (https://www.tamguide.com/subsection/3-1-2-tam-roles/), the standard TAMP ownership structure has:

- **TAM Champion**: Senior manager/executive advocating for TAM advancement
- **TAM Lead**: Coordinator of all TAM program activities; "head of the TAM unit" — this is the person who signs the TAMP and interacts with FHWA
- **Asset Stewards**: Division-level leads responsible for specific asset classes (bridge steward, pavement steward)

The TAM Lead is Aurigo's primary buyer persona for TAMP-related product conversations.

### Typical Production Phases (Synthesized from Evidence)

Based on APTech project scopes, Caltrans workshop series, GFT NJDOT description, MnDOT technical guide, and FHWA TAMP structure requirements, a representative 18-month production schedule looks like this:

| Phase | Duration | Key Activities | Key Deliverable |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 0: Kickoff and Work Plan** | Month 1–2 | Steering committee formation; asset scope confirmation; data source inventory; agency interview | Signed work plan; data gap list |
| **Phase 1: Asset Inventory and Data Assembly** | Month 2–5 | Pull condition data from BrM, HPMS, PMS; validate NHS completeness; multi-owner data collection for PA/CA/TX type states; gap fill | Verified asset inventory; data quality report |
| **Phase 2: Lifecycle Planning and Modeling** | Month 4–9 | Deterioration model selection and calibration; treatment cost library; life cycle cost analysis (LCCA); three-scenario modeling (current/minimum/target) | Lifecycle plan with condition projections by year and scenario |
| **Phase 3: Risk Management** | Month 6–10 | Risk register development; climate/extreme weather overlay; criticality scoring; 23 CFR Part 667 repetitive damage check | Risk register; climate vulnerability summary |
| **Phase 4: Financial Plan** | Month 8–12 | Funding source inventory (NHPP, STP, state match); multi-year allocation by asset class; performance gap quantification; investment strategy alternatives | Draft financial plan chapter |
| **Phase 5: Document Drafting** | Month 10–15 | TAMP chapter authoring; investment strategy narrative; methodology documentation; performance target tables | Full TAMP draft |
| **Phase 6: Review and Finalization** | Month 14–17 | Internal agency review; public stakeholder review; FHWA pre-submission review; response to comments; formal submission | FHWA-certified TAMP |
| **Phase 7 (annual): Consistency Determination** | 4–8 weeks annually (starts Year +1) | Compare current STIP investment to TAMP targets; flag drift; prepare consistency determination letter and exhibits | Consistency determination letter to FHWA |

**Note on phase overlap**: Phases 2–4 typically run in parallel, not sequentially. The lifecycle modeling feeds the financial plan directly. A tightly run engagement condenses months 4–12 significantly.

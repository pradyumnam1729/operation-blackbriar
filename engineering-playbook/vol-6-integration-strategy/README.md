# Volume 6: Integration Strategy

## Integration Philosophy

Aurigo Maintain is the **System of Intelligence** that sits above your existing EAM platform. It does not replace IBM Maximo, SAP EAM, Oracle EAM, Cityworks, or Infor EAM. It integrates with them.

This is a deliberate strategic choice. Agencies and organizations have invested years — sometimes decades — in configuring their EAM systems. They have trained their technicians on those systems. Their work orders, PM schedules, and vendor relationships live there. Asking them to abandon that investment in order to gain capital planning intelligence is a losing proposition.

Instead, Aurigo Maintain reads from your EAM, enriches the data with condition scores, RUL projections, ARV calculations, and risk scores, and surfaces capital investment intelligence that the EAM system was never designed to provide. When the customer is ready to consolidate, Maintain can absorb more functions incrementally — at the customer's pace, without a forced migration.

## The Three Deployment Modes

**Integrated Mode.** Maintain reads from the EAM system but does not write back. All work orders and PM schedules stay in the EAM. Maintain adds the intelligence layer. This is the fastest path to value and the lowest-risk starting point.

**Hybrid Mode.** Maintain reads from the EAM and writes back specific records — typically work order recommendations generated by the capital planning engine. The EAM remains the primary maintenance execution system. Maintain owns condition assessment, capital needs, and inspection workflows.

**Native Mode.** Maintain handles the full lifecycle — asset registry, inspections, condition, capital planning, and work order management. The legacy EAM is retired or kept in read-only archive mode. This is appropriate for greenfield agencies or those completing a full platform migration.

## How to Use This Volume

Start with `00-integration-overview.md` to understand the architecture and the philosophy. Then jump to the specific EAM guide for the system you are integrating (01 through 07). Use `08-rest-apis.md` when writing a new adapter — it defines the contract every adapter must implement. Refer to `14-canonical-data-models.md` whenever you are building field mappings.

The synchronization, conflict resolution, and canonical model documents are reference documents that all adapter authors must read before writing a single line of integration code.

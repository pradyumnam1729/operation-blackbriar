# General Availability Roadmap — Aurigo Maintain

## What GA Means

General Availability is the point at which Aurigo Maintain is sold without qualification to any enterprise customer that meets the standard commercial terms. Beta involves managed onboarding, close engineering involvement in customer configuration, and an implicit understanding that the product may change around the customer. GA means the product is stable, documented, secure, scalable, and supportable by a Customer Success team without per-customer engineering involvement.

GA is not defined by a feature list. It is defined by enterprise readiness: the platform has passed independent security audit, contractual SLAs are supportable with the architecture, self-service onboarding is possible for mid-market customers, and every customer-facing integration has documented APIs that Aurigo commits to supporting with backward compatibility.

The decision to declare GA is made by the VP of Product and Engineering Director jointly, based on the GA checklist below.

## Enterprise Readiness Requirements

### SOC 2 Type II Certification

SOC 2 Type II is non-negotiable for enterprise public sector sales. State DOTs operate under FISMA-equivalent controls and will require a SOC 2 Type II report before signing. The Type II audit covers a minimum 6-month observation period during which auditors observe that the controls documented in the Type I report are operating effectively.

SOC 2 audit preparation begins during Beta. Trust Service Criteria in scope: Security (CC), Availability (A), Confidentiality (C). Processing Integrity and Privacy are deferred to post-GA unless a specific customer requires them.

Key SOC 2 controls that require engineering work: access control review process (quarterly), encryption at rest (AES-256 for all customer data in RDS and S3), encryption in transit (TLS 1.2 minimum, TLS 1.3 preferred), penetration testing (annual, by approved third party), vulnerability management (critical CVEs patched within 14 days, high within 30 days), incident response plan (documented, tested annually), change management controls (which maps to the Human Approval Gates in this playbook), and data backup with tested restore procedures (daily backups, monthly restore test).

### 99.9% SLA

The 99.9% uptime SLA means no more than 8.7 hours of downtime per year. This is the standard enterprise SaaS SLA. Achieving it requires: multi-AZ RDS deployment (automated failover under 30 seconds), multi-AZ ECS deployment (container health checks, automatic task replacement), CDN for static assets (CloudFront), health check monitoring with PagerDuty alerting under 5 minutes, and defined maintenance windows (Sunday 2–4 AM UTC) during which planned downtime does not count against the SLA.

The 99.9% SLA applies to the core application. Report generation (TAMP PDF export, capital needs Excel export) and EAM sync jobs operate under a best-effort SLA for Beta, upgraded to 99.0% for GA.

### Enterprise SSO — SAML 2.0 and OIDC

Large government agencies use identity providers: Microsoft Entra ID (formerly Azure AD), Okta, Ping Identity, and Shibboleth (common in higher education and some DOTs). GA requires support for both SAML 2.0 and OIDC federation. The SSO implementation uses the Aurigo lambda-authorizer claim mapping layer so that SSO tokens are converted to the same internal JWT shape used by MVP authentication.

SSO configuration is self-service for Agency Admins: they enter their IdP metadata URL or upload the metadata XML, configure attribute mappings (which IdP attribute maps to which Aurigo role), and activate SSO. After activation, username/password login is disabled for that tenant. JIT (Just-In-Time) provisioning creates an Aurigo user record on first SSO login. SCIM provisioning is the GA-plus feature for automated deprovisioning (see below).

### SCIM 2.0 Provisioning

SCIM (System for Cross-domain Identity Management) enables the customer's IdP to automatically provision and deprovision Aurigo user accounts. When a field inspector leaves the agency, their HR system notifies the IdP, which notifies Aurigo via SCIM to deactivate the account — without an Agency Admin needing to do anything in Aurigo. This is a procurement requirement for enterprise accounts (CISA CISA Zero Trust Architecture guidelines require automated deprovisioning).

The SCIM endpoint implements the SCIM 2.0 spec: User resource (create, read, update, deactivate), Group resource (map IdP groups to Aurigo roles), and the /ServiceProviderConfig and /Schemas endpoints for discovery.

### Data Residency Options

Federal and state government customers increasingly require data residency guarantees: their data must reside within the continental United States and must not transit foreign data centers. GA requires: US-only deployment option (all AWS services run in us-east-1 and us-west-2, no cross-region replication outside the US), contractual data processing addendum (DPA) guaranteeing US residency, and the ability to designate a single-region deployment (us-east-1 only) for customers with state-level data residency laws.

EU residency is a post-GA feature for when Maintain expands internationally, but the architecture at GA must support it without data model changes (meaning data residency selection must be a deployment-time configuration, not a hard-coded assumption).

### Dedicated Tenant Infrastructure — Silo Model

For Tier 1 enterprise customers (annual contract value above $250K, or customers with specific contractual isolation requirements), GA offers a dedicated tenant infrastructure option. In the silo model, the customer's data is in a dedicated RDS instance (not shared with other tenants), their compute is in a dedicated ECS cluster, and their S3 bucket is isolated. The shared pool model (multiple tenants in one database with row-level tenant isolation) remains the default for standard and professional tier customers.

The silo model requires the IaC (Terraform) to support per-tenant resource provisioning. The application layer must route requests to the correct database connection string per tenant at the infrastructure level (not via application multi-tenancy logic).

### Full API Documentation

GA requires complete, accurate API documentation published at `docs.aurigo.com/maintain/api`. Documentation is generated from the OpenAPI spec (already maintained in Swagger). GA adds: authentication guide, pagination guide, rate limits, webhook documentation, changelog with version history, and at least one SDK (TypeScript, generated from the OpenAPI spec via openapi-typescript).

API backward compatibility is guaranteed for minor versions. Breaking changes require a major version bump and a minimum 6-month deprecation window with the old version remaining live.

## GA Feature Set

### Full EAM Connector Library

GA ships connectors for six EAM platforms in addition to Cityworks (MVP) and Maximo and SAP (Beta):

- **Oracle eAM** (Oracle E-Business Suite Asset Management) — REST API via Oracle Integration Cloud
- **Infor EAM** (formerly FAMIS/Hansen, common in municipalities) — REST API
- **MaintainX** (modern EAM for mid-market, API-first) — REST API
- **UpKeep** (cloud-native CMMS, growing in mid-market) — REST API
- **Accruent Vx Maintain** (common in facilities management) — REST + SOAP depending on version
- **Dude Solutions / Brightly** (SchoolDude, widely used in K-12 and municipal governments) — REST API

All connectors follow the same pattern: nightly asset import, inspection/work order sync, capital plan write-back. Each connector is implemented as an isolated adapter behind the `IEamConnector` interface in Infrastructure, making them independently testable and replaceable.

### AI Condition Prediction (Early Stage)

GA ships the first ML-based feature: a condition prediction model that forecasts asset condition at a future date with confidence intervals. The model is a Random Forest trained on historical inspection data from all tenants (anonymized, aggregated, with tenant consent). The model takes: current condition, asset age, asset class, asset subtype, last 3 inspection condition values, geographic climate zone, and average annual daily traffic (for pavements). Output: predicted condition at 2, 5, and 10 years with 90% confidence intervals.

The AI prediction is displayed alongside the deterministic Weibull model result. Users can see where the two models diverge and why (feature importance explanations using SHAP values, displayed as plain language: "this asset's condition is declining faster than average for its age and class in your climate zone").

The AI model is governed per the AI Governance policy: versioned, monitored for drift, with explained predictions. The model is not used for TAMP generation (which requires deterministic, auditable calculations) — it is used for planning horizon exploration.

### Mobile Apps — iOS and Android (Native, Offline-First)

The MVP mobile web inspection form works but has limitations: browser-level photo permissions are inconsistent across Android vendors, offline behavior depends on service worker implementation, and GPS accuracy for asset location varies. GA ships native iOS (Swift) and Android (Kotlin) apps focused on the inspection workflow.

Native app capabilities beyond mobile web: true offline mode (inspect without any connectivity, sync when back on network), device-native photo capture with EXIF data preserved (location embedded in photo), background sync (sync completes even when app is backgrounded), push notifications (inspection submitted, work order created, inspection approved), and barcode/QR code scanning for asset identification tags.

The native apps implement only the inspection-relevant screens: asset lookup, inspection form, defect recording, photo capture, and sync status. Capital planning, TAMP, and analytics remain web-only.

### Advanced Reporting — Custom Builder, Scheduled Delivery

GA adds a report builder: users can configure custom tabular reports selecting any combination of asset fields, inspection fields, RUL fields, and capital plan fields. Reports can be filtered, sorted, and grouped. Configured reports are saved and can be run on demand or scheduled (daily, weekly, monthly) with delivery to email or an S3 bucket.

Standard report library includes: assets due for replacement in next 3 years, inspection compliance rate (assets inspected within scheduled interval), condition improvement/degradation comparison year-over-year, capital expenditure vs plan variance, and work order sync status by EAM connector.

### Audit Log UI

The audit log (automatic via EF interceptor) has existed since MVP but was only accessible to engineers via database queries. GA exposes the audit log in the UI for Agency Admins: who changed what field, on which asset or record, at what time, from what IP address. Filterable by user, by entity type, by date range. Exportable to CSV for compliance reporting.

### Webhooks and REST API for Third-Party Integration

GA exposes a webhook system: customers can register webhook endpoints that receive POST requests when events occur in Maintain (asset created, inspection submitted, inspection approved, capital plan updated, work order synced). Each webhook is configurable for event type, with retry logic (3 attempts, exponential backoff) and a delivery log in the UI.

The public REST API (beyond what's used by the Maintain UI) enables customers to build their own integrations: GIS systems, business intelligence tools, financial planning systems. The API surface is the same API used internally, versioned and documented.

### Power BI and Tableau Connectors

Public sector customers universally use Power BI or Tableau for executive reporting. GA ships certified connectors for both:

**Power BI:** Custom Data Connector (.mez file) published to the Microsoft AppSource marketplace. Supports DirectQuery for real-time condition dashboards and Import mode for large historical datasets. Semantic model includes pre-built measures (portfolio average condition, total capital needs, % assets in poor condition).

**Tableau:** Tableau Web Data Connector (WDC) published to the Tableau Exchange. Extracts available for all core datasets. Tableau Server REST API support for automated extract refresh.

## GA Enterprise Readiness Checklist

Before GA can be declared:

- [ ] SOC 2 Type II report received from auditor
- [ ] Penetration test completed, all critical/high findings remediated
- [ ] 99.9% uptime achieved for 90 consecutive days in production
- [ ] SAML 2.0 and OIDC SSO tested with at least 3 different IdPs (Entra ID, Okta, Ping)
- [ ] SCIM provisioning tested end-to-end with automated deprovisioning
- [ ] Full API documentation published and reviewed by at least 2 external technical reviewers
- [ ] At least one Tier 1 customer live on silo infrastructure model
- [ ] Native iOS and Android apps published to App Store and Google Play
- [ ] All 8 EAM connectors tested with real customer instances (not just sandboxes)
- [ ] Power BI and Tableau connectors published to their respective marketplaces
- [ ] Incident response plan tested via tabletop exercise
- [ ] Data residency contractual DPA reviewed by legal
- [ ] Customer success onboarding playbook complete and used for at least 2 customer onboardings
- [ ] Self-service trial environment provisioned and tested
- [ ] Runbook for common operational procedures complete
- [ ] Disaster recovery tested (RDS restore from backup, full application recovery)

## Self-Service Trial

GA includes a self-service trial path for mid-market customers (agencies under 50 employees, data centers under 10MW, manufacturing plants under 500 employees). Trial customers can sign up at maintain.aurigo.com, create a tenant, import up to 500 assets from CSV, and use the full platform for 30 days without a sales conversation.

At trial end, the customer either self-serves to a paid plan via credit card (Professional tier) or enters the enterprise sales motion (Enterprise tier) by requesting a meeting. Trial data is preserved for 90 days after trial expiry, then deleted with 30 days notice to the registered email.

## Timeline

GA occurs approximately 18 months after Beta launch (36 months after MVP launch). The precise GA declaration date is held by the VP of Product based on the readiness checklist, not a calendar date.

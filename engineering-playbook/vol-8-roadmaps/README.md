# Vol 8 — Roadmaps

## What This Volume Is

This volume contains Aurigo Maintain's product and technology roadmaps. It covers the MVP, Beta, General Availability, AI features, scalability architecture evolution, enterprise readiness, longer-term innovation bets, and the research agenda that underpins them.

## Roadmaps Are Directional, Not Contractual

A roadmap is the current best view of where the product is heading and in what sequence. It is not a commitment. It is not a contract. It is not a promise made to customers, investors, or internal teams that specific features will ship on specific dates.

Roadmaps change. They change when customers tell us something different than we expected. They change when a technical constraint proves harder than estimated. They change when a competing priority emerges. They change when we learn from a Beta customer that the feature we planned for month 8 is actually the most critical thing for them right now and we should do it in month 4. These changes are not failures of planning — they are evidence that the team is listening and adapting.

When a roadmap changes, the change is recorded here with a brief note explaining why. This builds a historical log of decisions that is valuable for onboarding, retrospectives, and understanding the product's strategic trajectory over time.

## How to Read These Documents

Each roadmap document contains: the goals the phase is designed to achieve, the feature scope (what is in, what is explicitly out), success criteria expressed as measurable outcomes (not feature lists), a timeline with milestones, and dependencies on other phases or external factors.

Engineers should read the roadmap before beginning work on any significant feature to understand where that feature sits in the larger sequence, what it depends on, and what depends on it. Product managers should use these documents as the basis for customer conversations — never quoting dates to customers without checking with Engineering first.
